# Provenance: how the seats were filled, and the runs that went wrong

The record behind every choice in this shop, kept because the choices lean on it. Dates are real; the material and the reader are the origin project's and are not named here. The reader's verdicts are quoted where they were the basis of a decision.

## The audition (2026-08-22)

All candidates were tried on the same real material, one infrastructure change in the origin repository, so that every difference between candidates was the candidate's doing.

**Round 1, nine head-writer voices.** Take One wrote each voice from the author's reputation. Verdict: rejected wholesale; "Claude's register shines through strongly" (em dashes, the word "load-bearing", reversal frames). That failure triggered the research in `research.md`. Take Two rebuilt four voices (Spolsky, McKenzie, Feynman, Willison) with verbatim anchors, continuation framing and the lint gate; two more (Evans, Yong) were cut by the reader, because simplification loses the fidelity these explanations need. All four passed the gate on the first try. Verdict: Spolsky is the head writer ("He seems to have a very dominant voice here... he doesn't need all that much slamming"). The standing prompt was then written in his voice at the reader's request.

**Round 2, five structure advisors**, same pitch to each so that counsel differences were pure doctrine. McPhee named the pitch's shape correctly and lost the seat on reader fit ("there is something about them that just slips off my brain"); the letters format he was tried in (counsel, pushback, last word) was judged excellent and kept. McEnerney (each section answers a doubt or it is a diary) was preferred over Minto (answer-first; "My reader does not arrive holding a question. He arrives holding a sandwich."). Williams argued sentence-grain seams. Zinsser argued one point, killed a closing "Yet." and then, on the merits, resurrected it with the pine-boards condition. Verdict: Zinsser holds the seat.

**Round 3, four precision editors**, same draft to each, line pass only. Kernighan protected names and formatting, turned "Twelve. Not thirteen." into a verified concrete, and drew the boundary the seat inherits: "the code font marks the string, the deadpan is your business." Chen alone caught the metaphor's mechanical falsehood. Procida ruled twice against his own framework for the sake of the essay. Fowler found two concepts wearing one word and conceded the price of the fix openly. Deciding analysis: Procida's and Kernighan's value compresses into standing rules; Fowler's requires live judgment per text. Verdict: Fowler holds the seat, under the Kernighan boundary.

**The fabricated concrete.** The audition's flagship piece opened with machines "doing exactly what I told them to do eighteen months ago." The reader caught it at the very end: the infrastructure was months old. The number was invented for rhythm in the first pitch note and survived Zinsser, Kernighan, Chen, Procida and Fowler, because every check ran against a ground-truth sheet and the sheet was silent on timelines. Consequence: the veridicality step, owned by the writer, checked against the world by an agent with tools.

**Judgment principles from the audition.** Structure obeys instructions; texture leaks, so auditions must surface sentence-level texture. The pushback step is where quality comes from: an advisor never argued with is a rubber stamp, and a writer who always concedes is ventriloquism. A reader's "this slips off my brain" outranks a candidate's canonical status. Each seat should guard something no other seat guards.

Pin the date from the clock, in the brief.

## The serial redesign (2026-09-12, the same sitting)

The reader read the whole skill and ruled it was trying to be too many things: too many modes and dials. The original requirement, restated: the serial is gradual generation, almost Socratic, with the next step shaped by how the reader understood the last; generating every part up front, as run 5 did, is not in its spirit. His rulings, in order: probe the reader before generating anything (describe your understanding, "I don't know", or "I know this well"); remove the register dial; remove the short path and the full shop in favour of one incremental form that starts with an expert statement, serial 0, in the register of "a monoid in the category of endofunctors", a unit test of understanding and a pillar to walk toward; one structure consultation per session on a concept map, with parts generated on demand from it; per-part editing and checking, the goal being that the reader shows he understands in his own formulations, with clues of misunderstanding sought and met with more explanation, never counted as failure; the strict "part N of M" dropped for "Part N of ?", ordinals kept, detours announced and welcomed as the most desirable parts; a claim of knowledge answered with inquiry into what "this" is, so that known ground becomes the footing for analogy ("if you know map, I will teach you monad"); the learner ledger internal and never shown; the assessor never a Claude model. Pre-generating likely next parts while the reader reads was proposed, accepted, and then withdrawn by the reader as a speculative cache built before anyone had looked at usage patterns: "premature optimization, and what's worse a speculative cache." Ripped out. One tenet enshrined at his request: anchoring machinery runs once per session; forks share the prefix.

Two research passes and one debate fed the redesign. A subagent's survey of prior art (tutoring as curriculum-graph traversal, arXiv 2606.11744; misconception routing, arXiv 2606.23205; knowledge tracing from dialogue; Hayakawa's ladder of abstraction; cost-aware speculative execution, arXiv 2606.07846) produced the map-then-walk shape. A second subagent searched for a canonical question-asker with fetchable verbatim questioning and recommended Brian Lamb (425 questions across three Booknotes transcripts; one praise word, and it was the sign-off) with one Studs Terkel passage as the fourth exemplar; the reader: "love lamb/Terkel, great find." Serial 0 went to the crucible (Advocate GPT-5.6 terra, Skeptic Grok 4.5, Synthesizer Gemini 2.5 Pro): verdict revise. The Skeptic's strongest challenge, that compressing the map into a pillar is itself a structural judgment and the proposal handed it to one un-reviewed call in the seat that invents, was met by moving the pillar into the map consultation, where it gets argued at no extra call; the Advocate's hardening, a root-conformance gate in the check with canonical quotation as a mandated fallback, was adopted; the Synthesizer's flagged assumption, that a register instruction can hold the anchored voice down on a short declarative artifact, is the first thing to measure. The reader left the serial-0 ruling to the relay.

The editor bake-off ran the same afternoon, on the reader's word "run": run 3's DeepSeek draft and its twelve-item natural fault ledger, one Fowler and one Orwell letter per candidate family (DeepSeek as the in-family baseline, GPT-5.6 Sol, Grok 4.5, Qwen 3.7 Max, Mistral Large, Gemini 3.1 Pro as the control, Kimi K3), scored by Claude agents against the ledger. Two things went wrong on the way and are the record's to keep. Seven runners launched in parallel tripped OpenRouter's in-flight budget (each request reserves its whole completion ceiling against the account's credit); the remaining families ran one at a time with a retry loop, and the shop's rule from it is that letters to paid models go out sequentially. And Kimi K3 spent its entire 12,000-token and then 20,000-token ceiling on hidden reasoning and emitted nothing, both seats, and wrote both letters only when given 40,000, so a family's reasoning appetite is part of its seating cost. The letters were scored twice, by two independent sets of Claude agents: once by the relay before Kimi's letters existed, once by the main session after they did. The passes agreed on the precision seat item for item and disagreed on the tightening letters by up to four good cuts, Gemini's most of all (eight good and one bad in one pass, four and three in the other), because good against neutral is a judgment on whether a trim removed ornament or a word. The numbers and the seating rules are in `model-selection.md`, "The seats": Sol takes the precision editor's seat by a wide margin in both passes (five caught and six partial of twelve, no false alarm, no harmful fix), Kimi second once it had room to write, Grok third; Grok takes the tightening seat as the one placement that held in both passes and the only tightener to dissolve a costumed reversal frame, with Kimi's cleaner letter (five good, none bad) as the alternate; Mistral marked "do not seat"; the in-family DeepSeek editor sixth of seven, as the run-3 finding predicted. The assessor's seat gets a nomination (Sol) and keeps DeepSeek until real reader answers exist. The first pass's seating entry, which gave the tightening seat to Gemini on the numbers, was committed before the second pass and is superseded.

The first serial delivered under the new form, by the relay in the form's shape rather than by the shop (a chat answer to "what is the editor bake-off?"), drew one more ruling at its fifth part: the closing block's label had said CONTINUE OR ASK while the line beneath it offered a third move, "say run it". The label is now the exact set of moves; it grows to name an action and loses CONTINUE at the floor.

**Principles added.** A family that reads for facts is not therefore a family that cuts well; seat the two editing jobs separately. The editor's own letter goes through the gate: four of the seven precision letters failed it. A reasoning-heavy family can spend the whole completion ceiling before the first word, and a runner must treat an empty reply as a failed call, never as a short letter. A tightening score from one scorer is not a seating argument; the precision ledger scored the same twice, the cuts did not. The closing label is a contract, not a heading; a move it does not name is not on offer. A serial's next part is written after the reader replies, never before; a cache is built after the usage pattern is seen, never on a guess. A claim of knowledge is a footing to be measured, not a claim to be tested. The seat that asks is anchored like the seats that write, on questions actually asked, because a described questioner drifts to the mannered Socratic default the record already names.

## Run 6 (2026-09-12, the same day, later): the validation run of the redesigned serial

The redesign of the morning had never been run end to end, so issue #3 of mpj/mpj-skills asked for one concept through the whole form and named three things to measure. Subject: speculative decoding, framed as the question the reader would actually ask, how a served model emits tokens faster without a smaller model answering the request. It was chosen because its facts live in published papers, so the ground-truth sheet could be built by fetching abstracts and quoting them rather than by recall, and because the reader's own tooling has a fast mode documented as the same model with faster output. Thirty calls, $0.80, 86% of output tokens spent on hidden reasoning. Writer DeepSeek V4 Pro, structure advisor and precision editor GPT-5.6 Sol, tightening advisor Grok 4.5, interviewer and assessor DeepSeek V4 Pro, the check on the relay.

**The reader was not in the loop**, the run being authorized as autonomous work, so the interview's answers are a stand-in reader's, written by the relay in his register with each answer's ground truth pre-registered and hashed before any assessor saw it. Enough to exercise the machinery; not enough to seat the assessor, because the relay is a Claude model and the seat's rule is that it never is one. The interviewer's four probes are real output and are the thing to put in front of the reader.

**The pillar holds, which was the crucible's flagged assumption.** Serial 0 came back on the first call in plain declarative expert register: no scene, no analogy, no wisecrack, no rhetorical question, no second person inside the statement. The same seat in the same conversation opened Part 1 on "The prompt goes through in one pass" and Part 2 on "You noticed that batching helps", so the register switch is under instruction and is not the model's default leaking. Three faults, none of them register. The position line named the anchor author in the "who wrote it" slot; the conformance gate blocked delivery and the constrained rewrite came back "the writing shop", with the writer's own reason, that these documents are written in a borrowed voice and no living author's name belongs on them. Serial 0 introduced seven terms against a three-term rule that does not exempt the pillar. And the relay had to rule on whether serial 0 takes a SUMMARY block, because the skill says the pillar's one call formats the pillar and a TERMINOLOGY block while the checkpoint section says three blocks close every part.

**Per-part editing cannot see what a whole document shows.** Three per-part letters (seven, nine and nine items) against one whole-piece letter from a fresh instance on the same brief. The whole-piece pass found three things no per-part letter had, two of them impossible to see inside one part: memory-bandwidth bound defined one way in Part 1's checkpoint and another in Part 2's, three hundred words apart; Part 3's "every output token costs a weight read" quietly dropping the second of the two reads Part 1 established; and serial 0 carrying no bold defining occurrences at all, because the pillar is delivered before the precision editor is ever consulted, so the form as written sends the reader's first document out unedited. It also caught an unconditional claim that the per-part editing had itself introduced. The comparison is decisive in one direction only: the per-part editor had read the earlier parts and the whole-piece instance had not read the letters, so a whole-piece catch is a real hole and a per-part catch proves nothing. The two editors, same model and same brief, also contradicted each other once: the per-part letter proposed a sentence as a fix for a vague referent and the whole-piece letter asked for the same sentence deleted.

**The assessor arm, as a dry run.** Nine answers, three rounds, DeepSeek V4 Pro and GPT-5.6 Sol on the same brief in the same order. Neither raised a false alarm, and both caught all three planted defects, including the one that discriminates a careless reader, a confident and fluent claim that decoding at batch size one is compute-bound. Sol did it in roughly half the words. Two differences would matter live. DeepSeek wrote into the ledger a claim the sheet forbids ("Fast mode here uses the same large model with speculative decoding"), and the ledger is an input to the writer, so a false concrete there reaches the reader's prose; Sol hedged every mechanism claim. And as answers accumulated Sol moved a node to "Uncertain" and quoted the reader's own withdrawal, where DeepSeek kept the node at the original belief while quoting the same withdrawal. Both seats surfaced the same hole in the specification: "I don't know" is none of the four routes, and the skill separately calls it a complete and welcome answer. One of the relay's own labels was disputed by both seats, and the relay was probably wrong: an answer carrying a true observation and a false inference splits across two routes, which makes the "wrong" route nearly unreachable and leaves the misconception to be carried by the ledger's misconception section.

**The tightening seat's swap condition was watched and not met.** Grok caught both faults the gate and the precision editor had missed, a discourse cue opening a paragraph and a gloss interpreting the reader back to himself. One proposal was bad, a swap that would have turned "is evidence that" into "shows that" and undone the precision editor's own ruling; the writer declined it, cited that ruling, and the decline closed the item. That was the only line-level holdout of the run: against the precision editor the writer accepted twenty-five items of twenty-five across three letters, including two it was told would cost it something and invited to argue, while in the map consultation it held out on structure and won. This writer argues about shape and concedes every line.

**The gate, measured again.** Every part passed the four rules at every stage, including a draft of Part 2 that ran 304 words of prose against a 220 ceiling, opened a paragraph on a banned discourse cue and carried two reversal frames in costume. The editor caught the frames, the tightener caught the cue, and nothing mechanical caught the length: the writer's own count line said 190 where the relay counted 246. The register instruction holds the anchored voice down on a short declarative artifact, so the pillar can be plain and the same seat can be in voice a paragraph later. Per-part editing is the working pass and it cannot catch definition drift, a dropped distinction or a term rule broken three parts back; something has to read the whole. A pass that is delivered before any editor sees it is a pass with no editor. An editing seat can introduce the claim a later pass catches, so the last read is worth its call. A word count and a plain-ASCII check are the two cheapest rules the gate could take, and the writer's self-reported count is not one of them. Four routes do not cover a reader who says he does not know.

## The fusion, and the name (2026-09-13)

The reader read the two skills the morning after the validation run and ruled that they were one thing: "i'd like to fuse them and i think it should just be thought of as one thing inevitably." The text had already half agreed. `protocol.md`'s last section was headed "Serial delivery (the only delivery)", the serial's own opening said "There is no short form and no long form", and the two skills pointed at each other in circles, one a "Sibling of `serial`" and the other "Pairs with `writing-shop`". The split was a fossil of the morning when there were two paths and a register dial, both of which he had removed the day before.

He named a drift in the same breath: "i get the sense that we inadvertently created this to be something that generates web artifacts, which was never my intention. fundamentally this is just a way to run a kind of structured discussion with the user to create better understanding," with documents such as pull request bodies as a use of the same voice and not the core. The drift was real and it was concentrated in a few sentences an agent reads first. The old description opened "Write a piece of prose for a person"; the SKILL.md's middle heading was "How a piece is made"; the protocol ran "before a full draft", "after the draft". The unit of work in the prose was a piece, and the conversation was arranged around producing one.

**Three rulings settled the shape.** The masthead shrinks to two thin lines on a conversational turn, a position line and a ground line, with the title, the standfirst and the byline kept for a document somebody will hold onto: a masthead above every reply is what made a turn read as a published page. The machinery runs at two named settings, the interview and the ledger and the writer and the gate on every turn, the editor's letter and the tightening pass and the veridicality check when the text will be kept or when it carries concretes, because a part that goes through the whole shop costs several minutes between "continue" and the next screen of text. And the fused skill is called **`rung`**, from the move at its centre: the pillar stands and every part after it is one rung down the ladder of abstraction. Three names were offered and the reader took the recommendation.

**What the rename cost in vocabulary.** "Serial 0" lost its meaning once the skill stopped being called the serial, so the first delivery is now "the pillar", positioned as Part 0. The reader-facing unit stays "part", which is plainer English on a position line than "rung", and "rung" stays the word for the descent, which is where it was already doing work. The whole exchange is "the session", which the map consultation was already scoped to.

The same commit cleaned the shop's own files against the shop's own gate: hyphen bullets became asterisk bullets (a markdown `-` at the start of a line is a spaced hyphen to rule 1, and the gate is right to count it), and the em dashes left in headings went. What remains is logged at the end of `lint-gate.md`, and every one of those is the lexicon or the pattern being named rather than committed.

**Principles added.** A skill that has two names has two centres, and the file layout is the last place a settled decision arrives. Name the thing after the move the reader experiences, not after the shop that makes it. Furniture is a claim: a title and a byline over a conversational turn claim that a document was published, and readers believe the claim before they read the words. When machinery is too slow to run on every turn, say which parts run always and which run when it matters, or an agent will either spend four minutes on a throwaway line or ship an unchecked concrete.

## The delivery ruling: the shop writes, the agent marks its own edges (2026-09-13)

The first use of the skill on something real was a governing document in the reader's own private repository, which he had authorized the day before and could not remember. The map consultation ran, the structure advisor settled the map, the interviewer wrote four probes, and the orchestrating agent then wrote a report of what the run had found and posted it with the four probes at the bottom.

The reader read it and said: "the text above is already a bit opus-5-blabbery and I have a hard time believing that the message above isn't opus generated. the questions arent maybe?" Then the ruling: "rung does the copywriting and should be presented to the user as is. The harness agent (claude in this case of claude code) is allowed to fill in and do meta-commentary but should clearly delineate where the coding harness generation starts and where we quote the run subprocesses."

He was right about the message. Of roughly four hundred words, four short questions came from a seat and one clause was a near-quotation of the structure advisor, with its punctuation altered. Everything else was the orchestrating agent writing in its own register about a shop whose reason to exist is that its register is somebody else's. The agent had not claimed otherwise and had not said so either, and a reader cannot tell from the page.

**What went in the skill.** A section headed "Whose words reach the reader": what a seat writes is delivered as it came back, the agent may fill in and report and answer, and the boundary between the two is marked on the page with the seat named. The test is the reader's own question, "did you write that or did the shop", and a run that cannot answer it plainly has failed.

**Principle added.** The output of the machinery is the prose, so an agent that paraphrases a seat has run the whole shop and thrown away the product. Unmarked, the agent's own register is the only one the reader meets, and the reader's trust in every quoted line goes with it.

## The ground line becomes a question line (2026-09-13)

The same session that produced the delivery ruling produced this one, two parts later. The reader read a part whose second line was "So far:" followed by the eight terms grounded up to that point, and said: "i'm not so much a fan of the new 'so far' thing. it doesn't really help us. i know what we've talked about. what we need here is a clause that captures what the part is for: perhaps the question or possibly the question pair that the part is trying to answer in as clear a way as possible. it's okay if the question is confusing when you start reading the part but it should be clear by the time i reach the end of the part (given that i understand all the terminology and don't have to ask about it)."

The ground line was written for a reader arriving cold from another tab, and the only reader a session actually has is the one who has been in it. To him the line recited his own conversation back at him. Worse, it grew: by Part 2 it carried nine nouns and read as a bar of furniture, which is the failure the masthead ruling had already caught once in the same week.

**What replaced it.** From Part 1 on, the second thin line states the question the part answers, or the pair it answers. The pillar keeps the position line alone, because the question it answers is the session's. The reader's own test travelled with the ruling: the line may be opaque before the part and must be visibly answered after it, for a reader who holds the terminology. That gives the line a property nothing else in the frame has, which is that it can fail. A summary cannot be checked against the text that follows it; a question can.

**Principles added.** The frame is for the reader who is here, and he was here for everything before this part. A line that cannot be wrong is furniture. Where a part cannot be put as one question or a pair, it is carrying more than one part's work and it should be split.

## "Continue" is one word doing two jobs (2026-09-13)

By Part 5 of that session the reader had replied three times running with nothing the assessor could route: "continue, nothing confusing so far", "continue", and one message that was a request rather than an answer. The relay reported the starvation rather than hiding it, along with the two ways out: run the interviewer's follow-up even on a bare continue, which costs the reader a question he did not ask for, or say plainly that the map's edges drive the order until he says something with content in it.

He took neither and found a third. His words: "i think it makes sense that the interviewer's follow-up runs even on a bare, but maybe we actually change the 'continue' which is a little ambiguous. it's a little ambiguous what 'continue' means. perhaps we should just use the term 'i understand' (as in 'i understand part one') and then ask questions about it to fill in."

The diagnosis is exact. A reader who types "continue" may hold the part or may not, and the word carries neither, so the cheapest thing he can type is the one thing the ledger cannot use. "I understand" is a claim of knowledge, and this skill already had a rule for one: trusted, never second-guessed, followed at once by the sharpest inquiry into what "this" is. So the change costs no new machinery. It routes the most common reply into the rule that was already waiting for it.

**What went in.** The label becomes I UNDERSTAND, ASK OR GO ON. The third move is the relay's addition and it is there so nobody has to claim understanding in order to keep moving; a reader who says "go on" makes no claim, takes no follow-up, and is routed as the fifth route, no claim made. Under the label there are now two lines: the writer's, naming the moves, and the interviewer's one question about that part. Moving the follow-up inside the checkpoint is what makes the reader's proposal free, because the question stops being a turn of its own and the next part is still the first thing he sees after he replies.

**Principles added.** A move the reader makes is evidence or it is nothing, so name the moves after what they claim rather than after what they do next. Where a form already trusts a claim, route the common case into that trust rather than building a second mechanism beside it. And when the ledger is empty, say the map chose; do not let a deterministic walk of the graph wear the ledger's clothes.

## Authorship is not holding (2026-09-13)

Part 6 of that session leaned on three terms from a governing document the reader had written himself, the bound, the course and the account, and the relay told the writer to treat all three as held because the reader had written the document that defines them. And on which terms need giving back: "often the most complicated words are, or sometimes just simple but rare words like 'monad' or 'ontic.' i need to be reminded of even when i brought them in, just because it's been a few weeks and i've been mingled up in another project at work, that kind of thing".

**What went in.** A node is grounded by the ledger or by a claim the reader makes in this session, and by nothing else. An agent tempted to prune a node on authorship sends the interviewer at it instead, which costs one clause.

**Principle added.** Provenance of a term says nothing about whether it is in the reader's hand. In a working life where a person generates far more text than they can hold, having written something is close to no evidence at all, and the rarer and harder the word the less it means.

## The gate takes its two cheapest rules (2026-09-13)

## The driver is a contract and not a script (2026-09-13)
