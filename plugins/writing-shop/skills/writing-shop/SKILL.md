# writing-shop

A model told to write like an author writes an essay about that author, in its own register. Nine voices were auditioned this way on one real piece of material and all nine came back sounding like the same machine wearing nine hats. What fixed it was not a better description but the author's own sentences: three verbatim passages in context, and the instruction to continue them. Everything else in this shop exists because that alone was not enough either. Anchored prose still carried an invented "eighteen months ago" past five editors, because every editor checked terms against a sheet and nobody checked the world. And a piece that passed every mechanical gate was still handed back by its reader with one word, *sirligt* (Swedish: ornate, over-elegant), for sentences that announce the shape of the text instead of carrying it. The seats below are the answers to those three failures, in the order they were learned.

## The seats

The **head writer** writes everything, in one voice. The seated voice is Joel Spolsky, chosen by audition; the standing prompt is in `references/head-writer.md`, written in his voice, with the standing law the editors accumulated (one thing, one name; machine strings wear code face; a concrete that might be false is worse than none). The **structure advisor**, William Zinsser, is consulted before the draft on the one point, the lead, what gets cut and the exit. The **precision editor**, Martin Fowler, runs a line pass after it: definitions, one name per concept, distinctions that matter, and the order and marking of terms (a term's defining occurrence comes before anything leans on it and is set in bold, once; a word that survived the tightener on the merits owes a gloss), under Kernighan's boundary that the code font marks the string and the deadpan is the writer's business. The **tightening advisor**, Orwell with his fifth rule suspended, runs last and lightly: at most twelve proposals, cuts and short-for-long swaps only, hunting the ornate and the signpost. The **veridicality check** is requested by the writer and run by an agent with tools against whatever the claims are about: the repository, the API, the file, the calendar. The **gate** is four mechanical rules run on the finished file. The serial adds two seats: the **interviewer**, Brian Lamb, who probes the reader in three to five one-clause questions before anything is explained and asks the one follow-up after each part, and the **assessor**, a plain seat with no author voice that keeps an internal ledger of what the reader has shown he knows. Charters for the advisors and the interviewer, the assessor's rules, and the bench of candidates who lost their seats and why, are in `references/advisors.md`.

## How a piece is made

Read `references/protocol.md` before running it; this is the shape. Every writing or advising seat is set up the same way: it fetches three or more verbatim passages of its author's published prose, 300 words or longer with the punctuation intact, derives a ten-line bible of mechanical traits from what it actually read, receives a plain ground-truth sheet of every fact the text may assert, and has the hard rules restated in its own brief, because inherited context is not enough. Then the letters: the writer pitches, the advisor counsels, the writer pushes back and is told in so many words not to roll over, the advisor rules. Draft. The editor sends a numbered letter, the writer answers item by item, the editor rules on the holdouts. Revision. The tightener proposes, the writer accepts or declines each with a reason and holds the voice. Then the writer lists every concrete in the text (dates, counts, names, quotes, durations, who did what) and the check holds each against the world; anything unverifiable is cut or reworded as uncertain. Then the gate.

The orchestrating agent is the relay. Seats never speak to each other; every letter passes through it verbatim, and it is also where the facts get checked and the rulings logged. The letters are not theatre. The pushback puts trade-offs where the reader can see them, the advisor's last word makes a settled structure the revision can be held to, and staying in character keeps each seat inside its anchored texture instead of drifting back to the model's default. Collapsing the exchange into "just edit it" reintroduces the single-pass trade of style against correctness that the two-pass shape exists to avoid.

## What the shop knows about itself

The gate cannot see the fault the tightening seat exists for. Its four rules (em dashes and their spaced-hyphen cousins; the "not X, but Y" reversal frame; a banned lexicon; probable rule-of-three endings) are lexical, and discourse signposting has no remarkable word in it. `references/lint-gate.md` has the rules as patterns any agent can run; do not extend it with signpost regexes, which undercount a reader by about four times and teach the model to announce without the tell words. A deliberate exemption (a quoted lexicon, a term of art, a title with a dash in it) is logged with the piece, never edited away.

The line pass does not see the world. In three runs the editor caught none, four and three of the false concretes in a draft; the check caught nine, nine and eighteen. Terms and mechanisms are the editor's; dates and counts and attributions are the check's; and renaming a term of art is a factual act, because "false concretes" rewritten as "invented numbers" turned four true sentences false without touching a number. The ground-truth sheet is itself a concrete: it ages between runs, and "today" is the assumption nobody states, so pin the date from the clock in every brief.

Seat the models by family, not by rank. **The head writer sits on DeepSeek V4 Pro, always and explicitly**, by the reader's ruling of 2026-09-12: the shop exists to get prose out from under one model's fingerprint and mannerisms, and a Claude writer, however well it reads (Fable 5 reads better than Opus 5), puts the voice back under the family the reader is trying to escape; he would rather hold the seat under his own control. Every prose seat, the interviewer included, and the assessor sit on non-Claude models too (DeepSeek V4 Pro today; Grok 4.5 is licensed as an editing family, and a bake-off of editing families against a known fault ledger is staged); only the veridicality check may sit on a Claude agent or mirror the orchestrator. The cost of one family in every prose seat is on the record. Never let a Claude model rule on prose in this shop, and never let any model rule on its own family's prose without knowing the price: an in-family editor scores signposting as a virtue, and when writer and editor shared a model the editor caught a sixth of what a cross-family editor caught and echoed the writer's own error. The evidence, the bake-off that produced it, and the standing recommendations are in `references/model-selection.md`, dated, because they will go stale. What does not go stale: verbatim anchoring beats description by an order of magnitude; more than four exemplars buys nothing; a short, clean, anchored context for the writing seat may matter more than the model in it.

A seat that cannot lose an argument is not a seat. Every seat here has lost one to the writer on the merits, and those were the arguments worth having. A writer that accepts every proposal is ventriloquism, and one run produced exactly that: twenty-two of twenty-two accepted, including a cut that made a sentence stop being English. The provenance of every choice above, including the runs that went wrong, is in `references/provenance.md`.
