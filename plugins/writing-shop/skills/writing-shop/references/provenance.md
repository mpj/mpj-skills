# Provenance: how the seats were filled, and the runs that went wrong

The record behind every choice in this shop, kept because the choices lean on it. Dates are real; the material and the reader are the origin project's and are not named here. The reader's verdicts are quoted where they were the basis of a decision.

## The audition (2026-08-22)

All candidates were tried on the same real material, one infrastructure change in the origin repository, so that every difference between candidates was the candidate's doing.

**Round 1, nine head-writer voices.** Take One wrote each voice from the author's reputation. Verdict: rejected wholesale; "Claude's register shines through strongly" (em dashes, the word "load-bearing", reversal frames). That failure triggered the research in `research.md`. Take Two rebuilt four voices (Spolsky, McKenzie, Feynman, Willison) with verbatim anchors, continuation framing and the lint gate; two more (Evans, Yong) were cut by the reader, because simplification loses the fidelity these explanations need. All four passed the gate on the first try. Verdict: Spolsky is the head writer ("He seems to have a very dominant voice here... he doesn't need all that much slamming"). The standing prompt was then written in his voice at the reader's request.

**Round 2, five structure advisors**, same pitch to each so that counsel differences were pure doctrine. McPhee named the pitch's shape correctly and lost the seat on reader fit ("there is something about them that just slips off my brain"); the letters format he was tried in (counsel, pushback, last word) was judged excellent and kept. McEnerney (each section answers a doubt or it is a diary) was preferred over Minto (answer-first; "My reader does not arrive holding a question. He arrives holding a sandwich."). Williams argued sentence-grain seams. Zinsser argued one point, killed a closing "Yet." and then, on the merits, resurrected it with the pine-boards condition. Verdict: Zinsser holds the seat.

**Round 3, four precision editors**, same draft to each, line pass only. Kernighan protected names and formatting, turned "Twelve. Not thirteen." into a verified concrete, and drew the boundary the seat inherits: "the code font marks the string, the deadpan is your business." Chen alone caught the metaphor's mechanical falsehood. Procida ruled twice against his own framework for the sake of the essay. Fowler found two concepts wearing one word and conceded the price of the fix openly. Deciding analysis: Procida's and Kernighan's value compresses into standing rules; Fowler's requires live judgment per text. Verdict: Fowler holds the seat, under the Kernighan boundary.

**The fabricated concrete.** The audition's flagship piece opened with machines "doing exactly what I told them to do eighteen months ago." The reader caught it at the very end: the infrastructure was months old. The number was invented for rhythm in the first pitch note and survived Zinsser, Kernighan, Chen, Procida and Fowler, because every check ran against a ground-truth sheet and the sheet was silent on timelines. Consequence: the veridicality step, owned by the writer, checked against the world by an agent with tools.

**Judgment principles from the audition.** Structure obeys instructions; texture leaks, so auditions must surface sentence-level texture. The pushback step is where quality comes from: an advisor never argued with is a rubber stamp, and a writer who always concedes is ventriloquism. A reader's "this slips off my brain" outranks a candidate's canonical status. Each seat should guard something no other seat guards.

## Run 1 (2026-08-23): the shop on itself, every seat on one model

Subject: the shop and the pull request that carried it. Nine letters. Zinsser cut the writer's carried analogy on the writer's own reasoning, then lost a holdout to him on the merits and said so. Fowler pre-announced the item he expected to lose and lost it. The writer declined two of Fowler's nine items and two of Orwell's twelve, each on a stated ground.

Nine false concretes were caught and the line pass caught none: six by the veridicality check (a claim that review threads had been "engaged and closed" when none had been opened; a linter said to run "on every file" when it was a manual command; "a queue of one" when seven were open; an invented mechanism for a real fact; an essay title placed as the audition material when it was an output; one research pass where two ran), two when the orchestrator re-checked its own checker against the API (seventeen status contexts against a true nineteen), and one in the sentence the writer added to acknowledge those two ("wrong an hour ago"; under a minute by timestamp). Fowler's verdict on his seat, unprompted: "My seat sees terms. It does not see the world."

Eight days later the reader rejected the register anyway, in one word, *sirligt*, naming two sentences that tripped no gate rule. Consequences: the tightening seat (`advisors.md`), the bake-off and the model notes (`model-selection.md`).

**Principles added.** A seat that cannot be wrong is not a seat. The orchestrator's own checks need checking. A documented trait is a diagnosis, not a verdict on a particular sentence. The gate's silence is not evidence. And a check inherits its checker's assumptions, and "today" is the one nobody states: this run's documentation dated everything to the day of the piece for eight days because the orchestrator had told the checker a stale "today". Pin the date from the clock, in the brief.

## Run 2 (2026-09-12): a different family in the writer's seat; the serial form

Writer on DeepSeek V4 Pro as one stateful API conversation (anchors, prompt, prohibitions and sheet in the system message); Zinsser, Fowler, Orwell and the checker on Claude agents. Mid-run the reader ruled the serial form (the `serial` skill), and the writer re-pitched. Zinsser cut a borrowed analogy ("he cannot also learn TCP to admire a borrowed analogy"); the writer swapped the vehicle rather than cutting it, and Zinsser kept it on the condition that it not be cashed at the close.

Nine false concretes in the draft, a different mix: four were mechanism, and one went stale during the check (a commit count that moved twice in twenty minutes; the piece now prints no count, because a number that goes false within the hour is worse than none). The cross-family line pass caught four of the nine. The writer held out once, against Zinsser, and accepted ten of ten and twelve of twelve after; every accepted item was correct, so the concessions cost nothing here, and the pattern is the note.

## Run 3 (2026-09-12, later): every prose seat on one family, the checker on another

By the reader's ruling: writer, structure, precision and tightening all on DeepSeek V4 Pro, each its own conversation with its own anchors; only the veridicality check on a Claude agent. The draft carried eighteen false or unsupported claims, twice run 2's nine, and the mix moved to attribution: the reader was credited with rebuilding the voices, running the bake-off and writing its notes, all of which the shop's own agent did. One term swap did a quarter of the damage by itself: "false concretes" renamed "invented numbers" turned four true sentences false without touching a number. The in-family editor caught three of the eighteen and proposed a fix carrying a false count of its own. The revisions added nine more, caught by a second check: the ninth concrete "acknowledged the first eight" (two); draft placed before structure; the fault renamed "metadiscourse" against the shop's own file; a hypothesis promoted to a finding. One sentence came back after being fixed once and was struck by the relay, on the record. The relay put one falsehood there itself: the ground-truth sheet still carried run 2's seating, and the writer wrote it in verbatim. The writer accepted twenty-one of twenty-one proposals, including one that produced "came sirligt" and had to be shown Orwell's own rule six.

**Principles added.** In-family editing shows: the rule in `model-selection.md` held in the direction it was written for. Renaming a term of art is a factual act. A fixed error can come back, so the final pass is a full pass, not a delta on the changes requested. The sheet is a concrete: it was written for one run and read by the next.

## Run 4 (2026-09-12, later still): the shop on the ported skill, with the term rule

Subject, at the reader's request: how the shop and the serial fit into a working day. Same seating as run 3. The reader added a rule mid-run, now in Fowler's charter and the serial: terms arrive in order, once, and marked (the defining occurrence bold, a negotiated word owes a gloss, names in a list bold at first appearance). With that rule in his brief the in-family editor's letter was the strongest of the day: eight items, five of them terms used before their definition, plus a forbidden price, a wrong seat count and "invented numbers" where the shop says false concretes. The checker then found the draft telling the reader he could not intervene mid-run, on the day he had done so twice; the candidate uses narrated as events; and the reversal frame in four costumes the gate's patterns miss. The final full pass found two more (proposals miscounted as cuts; the checkpoint given the ground line's job) and the term rule broken in part 1 itself, where two seats were named before their definitions.

Two relay failures, both the orchestrator's: a silenced error sent an empty pitch to the structure advisor, who counselled on a blank page; a heading in bold defeated the letter extraction, and the advisor then invented a last word on a pushback he never received. Both letters are struck from the record. Check every letter before relaying it, and never parse a heading by its exact form.

**Principles added.** The term rule is an editor's rule and it pays at once: name the seat that enforces it and the seat catches it. A masthead written by the relay reads as machine-made; the writer writes it, in the voice. The relay's own failures are false concretes too, and they propagate faster than the writer's: an empty letter produces a confident reply.

## Run 5 (2026-09-12, later again): the full path with a Claude writer, and the ruling that closed that option

Subject as run 4, on the reader's request for a full-path comparison. The relay seated the writer on Claude Fable 5.1 (a fresh agent, anchors in its brief), reading `model-selection.md`'s "alternate head writer" line as permission; Zinsser, Fowler and Orwell each on DeepSeek V4 Pro in their own conversation; the check on the relay. Nine letters, eight writer turns, five advisor calls. The writer held out twice against Zinsser and won both, declined two of Fowler's nine and a half of two more and won every holdout, declined one of Orwell's four. The check found three false concretes in fifty-eight, one of which went false during the run: the pull request carrying the skill was merged seven minutes after the run began, and the draft said "open and not merged today". The skill's own file contradicted a second ("forty-nine lines" of reference implementation, above a 24-line code block). The full final pass found three terms used without grounding and two triad closers the gate's pattern cannot see. Gate clean, no exemption.

**The reader's ruling, on reading the seating:** the writer's seat is DeepSeek V4 Pro, always and explicitly. The point of the shop is to break free of Claude's fingerprinting and overbearing mannerisms; Fable is better than Opus and is excluded anyway, because the reader would rather hold the seat under his own control. `model-selection.md`, `protocol.md`, `head-writer.md` and `SKILL.md` now say so. Two further rulings followed in the same sitting. First, the relay's inference that a Claude model could still take an advising seat was overruled: only the veridicality seat may mirror the main agent or run on Claude; every prose seat is non-Claude. Second, the checkpoint's form: a closing paragraph in the voice did not say what it was (the reader: an inset box, not intuitively clear), and he asked for something self-descriptive on the model of the chapter ends in Deutsch's *The Beginning of Infinity*; the `serial` skill now specifies labelled TERMINOLOGY, SUMMARY and CONTINUE OR ASK blocks, matched to the page he sent (terms in italics, definitions of a sentence or two, bold capitals for the labels). This run's serial still carries the older checkpoint form.

**Principles added.** A file that ranks models is not a file that seats them; write the seating as a rule with its reason, or the next relay will read the ranking as permission. A concrete can go false between the sheet and the check even on the same morning; re-check the ones that live in a repository at the end, not the start.

## The serial redesign (2026-09-12, the same sitting)

The reader read the whole skill and ruled it was trying to be too many things: too many modes and dials. The original requirement, restated: the serial is gradual generation, almost Socratic, with the next step shaped by how the reader understood the last; generating every part up front, as run 5 did, is not in its spirit. His rulings, in order: probe the reader before generating anything (describe your understanding, "I don't know", or "I know this well"); remove the register dial; remove the short path and the full shop in favour of one incremental form that starts with an expert statement, serial 0, in the register of "a monoid in the category of endofunctors", a unit test of understanding and a pillar to walk toward; one structure consultation per session on a concept map, with parts generated on demand from it; per-part editing and checking, the goal being that the reader shows he understands in his own formulations, with clues of misunderstanding sought and met with more explanation, never counted as failure; the strict "part N of M" dropped for "Part N of ?", ordinals kept, detours announced and welcomed as the most desirable parts; a claim of knowledge answered with inquiry into what "this" is, so that known ground becomes the footing for analogy ("if you know map, I will teach you monad"); the learner ledger internal and never shown; the assessor never a Claude model. Pre-generating likely next parts while the reader reads was proposed, accepted, and then withdrawn by the reader as a speculative cache built before anyone had looked at usage patterns: "premature optimization, and what's worse a speculative cache." Ripped out. One tenet enshrined at his request: anchoring machinery runs once per session; forks share the prefix.

Two research passes and one debate fed the redesign. A subagent's survey of prior art (tutoring as curriculum-graph traversal, arXiv 2606.11744; misconception routing, arXiv 2606.23205; knowledge tracing from dialogue; Hayakawa's ladder of abstraction; cost-aware speculative execution, arXiv 2606.07846) produced the map-then-walk shape. A second subagent searched for a canonical question-asker with fetchable verbatim questioning and recommended Brian Lamb (425 questions across three Booknotes transcripts; one praise word, and it was the sign-off) with one Studs Terkel passage as the fourth exemplar; the reader: "love lamb/Terkel, great find." Serial 0 went to the crucible (Advocate GPT-5.6 terra, Skeptic Grok 4.5, Synthesizer Gemini 2.5 Pro): verdict revise. The Skeptic's strongest challenge, that compressing the map into a pillar is itself a structural judgment and the proposal handed it to one un-reviewed call in the seat that invents, was met by moving the pillar into the map consultation, where it gets argued at no extra call; the Advocate's hardening, a root-conformance gate in the check with canonical quotation as a mandated fallback, was adopted; the Synthesizer's flagged assumption, that a register instruction can hold the anchored voice down on a short declarative artifact, is the first thing to measure. The reader left the serial-0 ruling to the relay.

The editor bake-off ran the same afternoon, on the reader's word "run": run 3's DeepSeek draft and its twelve-item natural fault ledger, one Fowler and one Orwell letter per candidate family (DeepSeek as the in-family baseline, GPT-5.6 Sol, Grok 4.5, Qwen 3.7 Max, Mistral Large, Gemini 3.1 Pro as the control, Kimi K3), scored by Claude agents against the ledger. Two things went wrong on the way and are the record's to keep. Seven runners launched in parallel tripped OpenRouter's in-flight budget (each request reserves its whole completion ceiling against the account's credit); the remaining families ran one at a time with a retry loop, and the shop's rule from it is that letters to paid models go out sequentially. And Kimi K3 spent its entire 12,000-token and then 20,000-token ceiling on hidden reasoning and emitted nothing, both seats, and wrote both letters only when given 40,000, so a family's reasoning appetite is part of its seating cost. The letters were scored twice, by two independent sets of Claude agents: once by the relay before Kimi's letters existed, once by the main session after they did. The passes agreed on the precision seat item for item and disagreed on the tightening letters by up to four good cuts, Gemini's most of all (eight good and one bad in one pass, four and three in the other), because good against neutral is a judgment on whether a trim removed ornament or a word. The numbers and the seating rules are in `model-selection.md`, "The seats": Sol takes the precision editor's seat by a wide margin in both passes (five caught and six partial of twelve, no false alarm, no harmful fix), Kimi second once it had room to write, Grok third; Grok takes the tightening seat as the one placement that held in both passes and the only tightener to dissolve a costumed reversal frame, with Kimi's cleaner letter (five good, none bad) as the alternate; Mistral marked "do not seat"; the in-family DeepSeek editor sixth of seven, as the run-3 finding predicted. The assessor's seat gets a nomination (Sol) and keeps DeepSeek until real reader answers exist. The first pass's seating entry, which gave the tightening seat to Gemini on the numbers, was committed before the second pass and is superseded.

The first serial delivered under the new form, by the relay in the form's shape rather than by the shop (a chat answer to "what is the editor bake-off?"), drew one more ruling at its fifth part: the closing block's label had said CONTINUE OR ASK while the line beneath it offered a third move, "say run it". The label is now the exact set of moves; it grows to name an action and loses CONTINUE at the floor.

**Principles added.** A family that reads for facts is not therefore a family that cuts well; seat the two editing jobs separately. The editor's own letter goes through the gate: four of the seven precision letters failed it. A reasoning-heavy family can spend the whole completion ceiling before the first word, and a runner must treat an empty reply as a failed call, never as a short letter. A tightening score from one scorer is not a seating argument; the precision ledger scored the same twice, the cuts did not. The closing label is a contract, not a heading; a move it does not name is not on offer. A serial's next part is written after the reader replies, never before; a cache is built after the usage pattern is seen, never on a guess. A claim of knowledge is a footing to be measured, not a claim to be tested. The seat that asks is anchored like the seats that write, on questions actually asked, because a described questioner drifts to the mannered Socratic default the record already names.
